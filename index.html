01 / The mechanism

Why unmapped faults sit just outside the catalogue.

A surface-expression catalogue records where rock is exposed enough to map a trace. The faults the competition scores are by construction the ones the catalogue lacks, and the nearest places those can hide are the unmapped splays and stepovers of mapped systems — 300 m to 1.5 km off a mapped trace, close enough to share its damage zone. DrivenData staff confirmed that newly mapped geometry of an existing system counts as a new fault (thread 11536) and that known USGS/INGENIOUS pixels are masked out of scoring (thread 11516), so off-catalogue mass is the only mass that can earn credit.

Mechanically, the published index is DTI = T / (0.2T + 0.2F + 0.8G) with a 300 m triangular kernel applied as a maximum over neighbours. Redundant dots therefore add false-positive mass without adding true-positive credit: a binary dot at one kernel radius strictly dominates a graded map on the same support, and sparse spacing beats flooding.

Non-fault process that could mimic this: erosional and drainage lineaments, cultural or road linear features, and volcanic contacts that happen to parallel mapped traces inside the annulus. That is why the control is proximity-matched and why the delta, not the raw score, is the decision statistic.
