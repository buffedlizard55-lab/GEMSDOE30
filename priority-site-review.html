Why H33 led the supplied list

Metric-aware sparsity, not proven geology

The local historical reconstruction records that H33 retained 37,654 binary dots from a 40,199-dot GEMSDOE28 d2.8 base, removing 2,545 predictions within 2 pixels of the known catalogue. The exact support comparison reports zero differing pixels between H33 and that pruning rule. Under a distance-weighted metric with neighborhood-max credit, redundant nearby predictions can add false-positive penalty without adding equivalent true-positive credit; pruning can therefore plausibly improve the ratio.

This is a metric-mechanism explanation, not causal proof of hidden-label performance. The catalogue proxy cannot validate newly unmapped faults, and catalogue-buffer pruning could also delete real splays. The audit measured the historical raster; it did not verify organizer-side upload identity, hidden labels, or score attribution.
