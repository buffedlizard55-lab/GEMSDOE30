How it scored. Steps 1–3 only remove dots; the signal is the h19-5 "multiline-corroborated" topographic + geophysical lineament support, covering about 40% of the hidden truth at about 5.8× random-dot efficiency. Thinning to ~300 m-spaced isolated dots keeps most coverage while cutting false-positive mass roughly threefold (0.1922 → 0.2600). Removing dots within 200 m of mapped faults removed almost-creditless dots that still paid full false-positive cost (staff: predictions near known faults are fully penalised) — worth about +0.0178. H33-B2 won because it wastes the fewest dots on the same signal, not because it adds new information.

Can a unique submission beat 0.2778? In principle yes: leaderboard entries above 0.2818 exist, and ~0.30 needs roughly 0.145 hidden-fault hits per dot at ~44% coverage — a better-localised belief than h19-5 pruning. In practice not under this lane: a H33-class raster needs ~37,654 dots, which the lattice witness provably stops (≥84.92% overlap). A numerically unique raster must emit ≤ 22,163 dots with more than 30% outside the lattice neighbourhood; this session's unique artifact (20,000 dots, documented on the unique artifact page) shows no holdout skill above count-matched random dots (paired difference +0.000381, 95% CI [-0.002989, +0.004218]), so it is not competitive. Beating 0.2778 requires a new lane: assigned hypothesis, holdout evidence with paired CI excluding zero, complete-registry novelty check, and selector approval.

How to make a submission into the contest

Submission instructions

  1. Download the file. From any GEMSDOE site page get a single-band GeoTIFF (.tif), or a .zip containing a single GeoTIFF. The unique artifact generated here is downloadable from the unique artifact page.
  2. Check the gates locally first. `python scripts/validate_submission.py FILE --template data/raw/sample_submission.tif` must pass 12/12: float32, one band, EPSG:32611, shape 3730×3292, transform identical to the template, 100 m north-up grid, finite values in [0, 1] inside the footprint, and NaN/null outside it. Values outside [0, 1] produce the portal error "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]".
  3. Name and note. Give the submission a unique name and a short note (max 140 characters), e.g. "clustering with k=25", to tell submissions apart later.
  4. Upload. On the DrivenData submission page choose File to submit → the .tif (or .zip), enter the note, and submit. Up to three feedback submissions per week; one selected submission is scored on both label sets.
  5. Confirm status before uploading. A local format pass is not organizer acceptance. The registry duplicate-stop is active here, so no competitive slot is approved.

Recommended next work

What remains before a competitive submission

  • Assign one scientific lane and resolve the literal registry constraint (do not shrink the dot budget merely to evade the 70% rule).
  • Preregister whole-segment, spatially buffered hide-and-recover folds; derive catalogue-based features only from visible faults; audit each feature for leakage (AUC > 0.90 → leakage until proven otherwise).
  • Beat the same-protocol control on the holdout with paired 95% CI lower bound > 0; check surface and final dots against the complete registry; validate the exact saved bytes (this session's unique raster did not pass the skill gate).
  • Obtain separate slot-selector approval; stay within the weekly cap on the submission page.
