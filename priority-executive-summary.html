Why H33-B2 looks better in the supplied history.

The byte-level explanation is precision-oriented pruning, not a newly discovered geological signal. Reopening the files proves the path 44,090 d28 dots → 40,199 r1 dots → 37,654 H33-B2 dots. The first pruning removes 3,891 dots within 1 pixel of the visible catalogue; H33 removes another 2,545 dots within 2 pixels. The H33 set is exactly that distance-pruned r1 subset. GEMSDOE25 / GEMSDOE30 are value-identical; GEMSDOE28 r1 / GEMSDOE31 are also value-identical inside the footprint.

With DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2 FP + 0.8 FN), discarding low-yield dots can reduce FP more than it reduces TP. This is consistent with the owner-supplied H33 claim 0.2778. It does not identify hidden TP/FP uniquely or prove causation: visible-catalogue proximity may correlate with real unmapped faults, and no organizer submission receipt is present. GEMSDOE36 adds exactly 1,200 dots to H33, alongside the supplied decline to 0.2750; those additions are not evidence of a better signal.

The newer historical GEMSDOE28 rung has 37,660 dots and is not the 40,199-dot r1 intermediate.
