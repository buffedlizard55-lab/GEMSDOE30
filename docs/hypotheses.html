New register (2026-10-03, session 30): H-34 — four candidates that target faults a surface catalogue cannot contain

Two measured facts bound what can still move the score. (1) Emission is nearly solved: the published index rewards sparse binary dots, and on the component holdout this session a metric-optimal emitter (EDGE, src/gemsdoe30/emitter_opt.py) beats every belief-ordered emission family on the same belief field — no emitter change creates coverage the field does not support (score-ceiling analysis). (2) The catalogue is not the target: the organizer masks USGS/INGENIOUS pixels, and this session reproduced a sign inversion — the catalogue proxy ranks dot_thin(H19-5, d) as d1.5 > h19-5 > d2.8 while the owner-reported leaderboard ranks them the other way. Every H-34 candidate therefore targets faults that a surface-mapping catalogue is structurally unable to record, uses free official data, and can be tested without hidden labels. None is validated; none may consume a weekly slot. Full detail: Markdown register.

1 · H-34-01
Substrate-concealment prior (observability model)		 USGS SGMC state geologic-map polygons (map-unit age + lithology → surficial/young-cover mask) crossed with the relief bands. Signature: an observability weight, not an edge filter — where young cover and basin fill hide surface expression, inside the known structural grain. A catalogue is a mapping-effort product: it records where rock is exposed enough to map a trace. Faults under basin fill and Quaternary cover are missing from any surface catalogue, while the organizer's new faults are by construction what the catalogue lacks. Every prior hypothesis here used an evidence field (scarp, magnetic edge, corridor, proximity). None modelled the selection process that produced the labels. A concealment prior re-weights where to spend existing evidence instead of adding a detector. +0.002…+0.015
Low cost: archives already downloaded and hash-verified; the fetch script rasterises only linework and must be extended to polygon units.		 USGS SGMC — public domain, consistent lithology/age/GIS structure. Verified this session: NV.zip 69,056,094 B and CA.zip 24,977,406 B recorded OK in data/external/external_receipt.json via the repository's GitHub-Actions bridge. Distance-matched control is mandatory (the prior must not reduce to "far from the catalogue"): concealment-weighted emission vs the same emitter on a proximity field and on a count- and distance-matched random field; require ≥ +0.005 over same-run best, ≥ 3/4 spatial folds positive, fresh-draw confirmation, lithology-stratified null reported.
2 · H-34-02
Potential-field depth lineaments (tilt + Euler deconvolution)		 GeoDAWN airborne magnetic grids (or the competition magnetic bands). Signature: upward continuation → tilt-angle derivative → 3-D analytic signal → Euler depth; keep lineaments with inverted source depth ≥ ~0.5–1 km. A buried magnetic source with no surface rupture produces no scarp, no mapped trace and little topographic lineament: invisible to a surface catalogue while still focusing permeability and heat. Prior and registered work used magnetic values or their horizontal gradient. No inspected code inverts for depth; the depth filter is exactly what separates "already mapped" from "cannot be mapped". +0.002…+0.012
Medium: transforms are cheap; owner-derivative u8 quantisation and band lineage must be re-verified against the official grids first.		 USGS GeoDAWN, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ — verified this session at ScienceBase: Walker Lane / western Great Basin, 149,030 line-km, release includes geoTIFF geophysical grids. Sandbox cannot reach sciencebase; the proven runner bridge is the transfer path. Preregister the depth cutoff and tracker. Depth-filtered arm must beat (a) the same transform without the depth filter and (b) a spacing/distance-matched control on the buffered component holdout; ≥ +0.005, ≥ 3/4 folds; reject if driven by flight-line artefacts (rotated-kernel null).
3 · H-34-03
Drainage χ-transform disequilibrium and along-strike ksn		 USGS NHD flowlines + 3DEP DEM. Signature: χ-transform disequilibrium across a candidate trace plus strike-aligned normalised channel-steepness (ksn) anomalies at a fixed concavity index. Fluvial networks integrate uplift and record deformation where the scarp is degraded or buried; NHD/3DEP come from a different programme than the fault catalogue, so agreement is independent evidence. The registered-but-unrun H-31-03 searched for channel offsets/beheaded channels/knickpoint chains qualitatively; χ/ksn is a quantitative, area-normalising transform. Nothing in the repository computes χ or ksn. +0.002…+0.010
High: DEM conditioning, flow routing, profile extraction, lithology controls.		 USGS TNM Products API — verified this session: 35 NHD product records for a test bbox with direct public URLs (prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com); California state FileGDB 982,386,104 B; 3DEP public domain. Full-ROI transfer not yet verified — not runnable yet. Blocked until clipped NHD + 3DEP data are on disk and checksummed. Then preregister concavity index and χ threshold; beat slope-only and drainage-density-matched controls (≥ +0.005, ≥ 3/4 folds) with whole-basin holdout.
4 · H-34-04
Heat-flow anomalies and borehole-gradient discontinuities		 IHFC Global Heat Flow Database (release 2024) site values + GDR well/spring records. Signature: heat-flow anomaly (W m⁻²), its spatial gradient, and borehole depth-gradient discontinuities aligned along strike. A permeable fault zone focuses convective heat transport, so heat flow can be anomalous above a structure with no surface rupture; heat-flow sites come from drilling programmes, not fault mapping. Prior thermal work used spring temperature populations, geothermometer chemistry and 2 m probes as point features. Heat flow is a different observable with a different sampling design. +0.001…+0.008
Medium; low statistical power — count ROI sites before running.		 IHFC Global Heat Flow Database — verified this session: release 2024 has ~91,182 points (54,553 continental), downloadable via DOI 10.5880/fidgeo.2024.014; quality documented in doi:10.1016/j.tecto.2023.229976. Count ROI sites first; if fewer than ~50 in the footprint, report as exploratory only. Otherwise hold out whole site clusters, compare anomaly+alignment vs temperature-only and count/distance-matched random controls; ≥ +0.005 and ≥ 3/4 folds, else stop.

Ranking logic. H-34-01 first: it is the only candidate that models why the labels are missing, reuses verified archives, and has a cheap decisive control. H-34-02 second: depth-localised magnetic sources are a textbook blind-fault signature with official grids available. H-34-03 has the best physical independence but the highest cost and an unverified transfer. H-34-04 is cheapest to start and the most likely to be underpowered in one 350 × 330 km footprint. Next action: H-34-01, because its data are already on disk.

Evidence boundary

The shortlist excludes tested variants in the inspected repository and public owner summaries. It cannot establish absence of private experiments. Recheck exact layer manifests and provenance before preregistration; if a candidate duplicates a prior arm, replace it before looking at scores.
