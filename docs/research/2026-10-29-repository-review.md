## What the 0.2778 H33-B2 report does—and does not—show

Reopening the historical rasters supports this **byte-level lineage**:

- D2.8 family: 44,090 dots.
- Earlier `r1`: 40,199 dots, an exact subset after removing 3,891 dots at 1-pixel catalogue distance.
- H33-B2: 37,654 dots, an exact subset of `r1`; it removes a further 2,545 dots whose catalogue distances are 1.414–2.0 pixels.
- The GEMSDOE36 PINN raster is H33-B2 plus 1,200 additional dots; its reported value is 0.2750.

Thus the 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 sequence adds no new emitted dots or independent geological layer in these exact raster transitions; it is consistent with pruning near-catalogue dots whose remaining unmasked false-positive cost can exceed the recall/kernel support they contribute. The [organizer's scoring clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516) says known-fault masking is pixel-exact, so a dot merely 1–2 pixels from a mapped pixel is not thereby masked; see also the [official problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/). Adding dots can also reduce a precision-weighted metric, consistent with the lower reported PINN value.

That is a plausible mechanism, **not an identified causal explanation of the hidden score**. The 0.2778 is an owner-reported scalar, not a receipt. No hidden truth, verified organizer submission record, or TP/FP decomposition is present. Raster subtraction cannot establish whether the deleted dots were false positives, whether nearby retained dots preserved all true-positive kernel credit, or how much of any score change came from this prune. Do not reproduce the obsolete numerical inversion or claim the hidden labels can be reconstructed from the scalar.

**Could a genuinely different method improve on 0.2778?** In principle, yes: the byte lineage suggests headroom could come from adding well-supported concealed-fault recall rather than further catalogue-halo deletion. But the repository has no comparable organizer-confirmed estimate demonstrating an improvement, and no numeric expected score is justified. A `HOLDOUT-DTI` result can compare registered catalogue hide-and-recover arms under one evaluator; it is not an organizer score and cannot by itself forecast the hidden leaderboard. No new lane is authorized here.
